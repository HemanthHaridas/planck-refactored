## Why it matters

Coupled cluster is not variational, so the energy alone gives no densities
and no cheap derivatives. The Λ equations make the CC energy stationary by
adding a left-hand state ⟨0|(1 + Λ):

    L(T, Λ) = ⟨0| (1 + Λ) H̄ |0⟩,   H̄ = e^{−T} H e^{T}

Stationarity in Λ gives back the T equations; stationarity in T gives the
Λ equations, one linear set per rank. With T and Λ converged, every
first-order property is an expectation value of (1 + Λ) and e^{−T} … e^{T}.

| Consumer | Needs beyond Λ | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|
| Unrelaxed CC 1- and 2-RDMs, dipoles, natural orbitals | the densities (built) and their Planck kernels | here, 3d–3e |
| ΛCCSD(T), CCSDT(Q)_Λ, CR-CC(2,3) | a Λ-weighted left vector in the perturbative energy | `docs/CCSDT_Q_SCOPE.md` |
| Analytic CC gradients | the CC orbital Lagrangian from the RDMs and a Z-vector | own scope; the RMP2/UMP2 gradients have the Z-vector machinery (`rhf_response`, `uhf_response`) |
| EOM-CC left states and transition properties | EOM itself | own scope |

One generator gives all of these at any rank: hand-written Λ-CCSD exists in
every code, Λ-CCSDT and Λ-CCSDTQ almost nowhere.

## The route: differentiate the Lagrangian

The T equations come from the diagram engine (`PLANCK_CC_ENGINE` defaults
to `diagram`, 3.0 s against Wick's 615 s at CCSDT,
`docs/CCGEN_DIAGRAM_REPRESENTATION.md`). The Lagrangian is built from them,

    L(T, Λ) = E(T) + Σ_μ λ_μ R_μ(T)

and the Λ residual is its derivative, ∂L/∂t_ν = ∂E/∂t_ν + Σ_μ λ_μ ∂R_μ/∂t_ν,
with ∂R_μ/∂t_ν = ⟨μ|[H̄, τ_ν]|0⟩. Each Λ term is an L term with one t factor
removed and its indices freed. The terms where Λ touches only τ_ν (linked
but not connected) come out of the algebra, so no connectivity rule is
needed. The densities are the same derivative in another variable:
∂L/∂f and ∂L/∂v.

Routes not taken: Wick projection of ⟨0|(1 + Λ)[H̄, τ_ν]|0⟩ (needs a
de-excitation operator, a ket excitation with free indices and a
linked-but-not-connected projection, at Wick's cost), and Λ vertices in the
diagram encoding (more work than differentiation for the same terms).

**Conventions.**
- λ_n is `l<n>` with indices (occ..., vir...), e.g. `l2(i,j,a,b)`: PySCF's
  `l1[i,a]`, `l2[i,j,a,b]`. Each residual key gets its own λ: `l2`,
  `l4_aaabaaab`, `l2_abab`.
- Weight of λ·R: 1/(n!)² for GCC (PySCF's normalization; 1/n! fails the
  gate), 1/(n_α! n_β!)² per UCC block (the GCC Lagrangian restricted to
  stored blocks, so UCC λ is PySCF's), 1/(n!)² for RCC (any weight is valid
  there; it only rescales λ).
- `differentiate` returns G = ∂L/∂T with every element independent, in t's
  (vir..., occ...) layout. The Λ residual is G symmetrized over t's own
  symmetry: antisymmetrized for GCC and same-spin UCC blocks, the pair
  symmetry for RCC.
- **Canonical Fock is enough for Λ; the densities need the f_ov terms.**
  Production generates with `canonical_fock=True`, which drops every term
  carrying an f_ov factor (zero for a canonical reference). ∂/∂t removes a
  t factor, not f_ov, so the derivative of a dropped term is still zero:
  the Λ residual kernels come from the same equations as the T kernels.
  The densities differentiate with respect to f itself, so ∂L/∂f_ov removes
  the f_ov factor and leaves a nonzero term (2 f_ia t_ia in the spatial
  energy gives 2 t_ia, the leading piece of D_ov). The density terms are
  therefore generated with `canonical_fock=False` and evaluated with
  f_ov = 0.

## How each piece is built and gated

### Step 1: spin-orbital Λ

`lagrangian_terms(eqs, "gcc")` contracts λ onto each residual term;
`differentiate(lag, name)` removes each factor `name`, renames every dummy
to a fresh name so the freed indices can take the pool names, then
canonicalizes and merges.

- **Complex step** (ε = 1e-20i): Σ G·D equals dL/dε to ≤ 3.7e-15 at every
  rank of CCSD and CCSDT; dropping one G term fails it.
- **PySCF.** At GCCSD's converged t and λ, the antisymmetrized G is ≤ 3e-11
  on water STO-3G and C1 H2O2 6-31G; 1 % on λ2 makes it nonzero; a 1/n!
  weight fails both.
- **Found:** the PySCF gates use the f_ov-keeping equations
  (`canonical_fock=False`) because of the reference, not because of Λ.
  PySCF's SCF stops with |f_ov| = 1.1e-8 on H2O2 6-31G, and with the f_ov
  terms dropped the rank-1 residual sat exactly there (1.06e-8) at any λ
  tolerance. An exactly canonical reference, as in Planck, does not see it.
- Term counts (canonical Fock, after merge): Λ-CCSD 54 / 32 (λ1 / λ2);
  Λ-CCSDT 193 / 91 / 32; Λ-CCSDTQ 752 / 262 / 91 / 32, generated in 1.3 s.

### Step 2: RCC and UCC

Differentiate the adapted equations directly (`lagrangian_terms(eqs,
"rcc" | "ucc")`), so λ never enters the spin adapters.

- **UCC.** At PySCF UCCSD's converged t and λ, every block's residual is
  ≤ 1.0e-10 (CH3 STO-3G, water⁺ 6-31G); the 1/(n!)² weight on the αβαβ
  block misses by 8e-2.
- **RCC.** The spatial λ is not PySCF's, so the gate is the unrelaxed
  energy derivative: Λ solved by GMRES at PySCF RCCSD's t, then dL/dε for a
  random Fock perturbation against a four-point FD of PySCF's E_corr with
  `eris.fock += εX`. X has occupied–virtual elements, so this gate also
  uses the f_ov-keeping equations. Water STO-3G 4.8e-10, C1 H2O2 6-31G 5.6e-9 (the FD's
  noise); dropping one λ2 term misses by 9e-2. The spatial E_corr and T
  residuals reproduce PySCF at its t first, which pins the conventions.
- **Complex step** exact for every RCC and UCC block through CCSDT. RCC
  Λ-CCSDT 795 / 1141 / 345 terms, UCC up to 1133 per block, under 3 s.

### Step 3a: unrelaxed densities

`density_terms(lag, spin)` returns ∂L/∂(f or v) per space block
(`differentiate_blocks`), from the f_ov-keeping equations (see
"Conventions": D_ov is the derivative of exactly the terms canonical Fock
drops). Total RDMs follow by the
chain rule through E = E_ref(h, g) + L(f(h, g), v(g)), in PySCF's
convention E = Σ h D + ½ Σ (pq|rs) Γ (UCC: ½ αα, 1 αβ, ½ ββ).

| | 1-RDM | 2-RDM | E from RDMs − E_CC |
|---|---|---|---|
| RCC water STO-3G (our spatial λ) | 7.8e-11 | 1.6e-10 | −2.6e-13 |
| RCC C1 H2O2 6-31G | 1.2e-10 | 2.5e-10 | 1.7e-13 |
| UCC CH3 STO-3G (PySCF's λ) | 1.7e-18 | 1.1e-16 | −7.2e-12 |
| UCC water⁺ 6-31G | 1.7e-18 | 1.1e-16 | −7.7e-12 |

The RCC rows compare densities from our λ with PySCF's from its λ, so they
also show the densities do not depend on the λ parametrization. Dropping
one density term misses by 1e-4 to 8e-2.

## Left: Λ and densities in Planck (3b–3e)

- **3b, emitter.** Λ residual kernels that read t and λ: one more
  amplitudes-like argument and `l<rank>` view bindings beside the `t<rank>`
  ones (`_amplitude_view_bindings`). Λ terms are binary contractions after
  dressing like the T terms, so dressing, the factorizer and the GEMM
  lowering apply unchanged. Restricted rank 2 first (generated in every
  build), UCC through the same path.
- **3c, solver.** The Λ equations are linear; the Jacobi/DIIS harness
  (`run_generated_arbitrary_order_iterations`) runs with T frozen. The
  diagonal is the orbital-energy denominators times a per-rank constant set
  by the λ weight, derivable at generation time. Start λ from t. Measure a
  Λ iteration against a T iteration; it should cost about the same.
- **3d, densities.** Kernels from `density_terms` (generated with
  `canonical_fock=False`, evaluated with Planck's f_ov = 0), and the
  chain-rule assembly above in C++. The Λ kernels (3b) use the production
  canonical-Fock equations.
- **3e, driver.** An input keyword (name to decide), unrelaxed dipole and
  natural occupations. Gates through the regression runner: PySCF's RDMs,
  the energy identity, and for CCSDT and up, where no reference code is at
  hand, dE/dε by central differences of the CC energy under an integral
  perturbation against Σ Γ X.

## Step 4: consumers (each its own decision)

- **ΛCCSD(T)**: (T) with a Λ-dependent left vector, against PySCF's (T)
  gradient machinery where it applies.
- **CCSDT(Q)_Λ, CR-CC(2,3)**: `docs/CCSDT_Q_SCOPE.md`.
- **Analytic CC gradients**: their own scope, after the densities pass in
  Planck. New piece: the CC orbital Lagrangian from D and Γ. References:
  PySCF `grad.ccsd`, `grad.ccsd_t`.

## Risks still open

- **Conventions in C++.** A wrong factor or transpose still converges, to
  a wrong λ; the CC energy does not depend on λ, so only the density gates
  catch it.
- **Dressing.** The retired dressed spin-adapted route
  (`docs/CCGEN_DRESSING_AND_SPIN_ADAPTATION.md`) shows two correct
  transforms composing wrongly. The Python gates cover differentiation
  after adaptation; dressing the Λ terms needs its own gate after emission.
- **Generating the densities from canonical-Fock equations** loses D_ov
  with no error (the code runs, the occupied–virtual block is just missing,
  and the dipole with it). The 3d gates must check D_ov. Λ for ROHF
  references, where f_ov ≠ 0, inherits `docs/ROHF_CORRELATION_SCOPE.md`.
- **Memory.** λ doubles amplitude storage: at CCSDT, t3 and λ3 are both
  o³v³.

## Not in this scope

EOM-CC and linear-response CC (they share the left-hand operator algebra),
orbital-relaxed properties and gradients, ROHF references, frozen core, RI.
